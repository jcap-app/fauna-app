This Privacy Policy describes how your personal data is collected, used, and handled when you use the Fauna mobile application (“Fauna” or the “App”).

1. Who we are (Data Controller)

Fauna (“Fauna,” “we,” “us,” “our”) is a mobile application that uses artificial intelligence to help people interpret animal behavior. The data controller responsible for your personal data is Jason Capostagno, an individual sole proprietor based in New York, USA (to be replaced with “Fauna LLC” once the LLC is formed).

  • Contact / data-protection requests: fauna.app.support@gmail.com
  • Postal address: available on request at the email above

We do not have an establishment in the European Union and have not appointed an EU/UK representative under Article 27 GDPR; given the nature and scale of processing we rely on the Article 27(2) derogation where applicable. If our processing changes, we will appoint a representative and update this policy.

2. Summary (the short version)

  • Fauna is local-first: your logs, photos, audio, video, notes, and daily check-ins live on your device.
  • To make sync, teams, animal-sharing, community, and account-recovery features work, Fauna automatically creates a lightweight anonymous account on our servers (hosted by Supabase). We therefore do operate a database, and the data it holds is linked to your anonymous account (see Section 3 for exactly what is stored).
  • To generate an interpretation, the content you submit is sent off your device to a third-party AI provider (Anthropic) by way of a processing proxy (hosted on Cloudflare). This involves a transfer to the United States.
  • Community observations are contributed by default. If you leave this on, Fauna sends the behaviour note you wrote and the interpretation the AI returned, with the species and date, to our servers to build shared community statistics. It is pseudonymous, not anonymous: it is linked to your anonymous account identifier (never your real name), which is what lets us delete it if you ask. Your photos, audio and video are not included. Because your note is free text, please don’t type anything into it you would not want in a shared record. You can turn this off at any time in Settings → Privacy.
  • Atlas contribution is OFF unless you turn it on. If you switch it on, Fauna contributes measurements computed from your recordings — pitch, duration, spectral shape, call rate — with the species, the context you chose and the date, to build shared reference data about how animals of each species vocalise. Your recordings and your free-text notes are never included. These records are pseudonymised, not anonymous: they stay linked to your account so we can delete them if you ask. Turn it on or off any time in Settings → Privacy.
  • Publishing to a community feed is separate and always per-post. Contributing measurements never publishes anything.
  • We collect content-free product-usage analytics (which screens and features you use, counts, your tier — never your notes, your animal’s name, or media) to improve the app. It is on by default and you can turn it off anytime in Settings → Privacy.
  • We do not sell your personal data, and we do not use it for third-party advertising or tracking.
  • Fauna does not provide veterinary or medical advice. See our Terms of Service.

3. What data we process

Fauna is local-first, but some data is stored on our servers (Supabase) so that sync, teams, sharing, community, account recovery, and crash-fixing can work. Data we store on our servers is linked to your anonymous account identifier.

a) Your account. When you first open Fauna, we create a lightweight anonymous account — no email or password required. It has a random account identifier and a shareable Fauna ID so teammates and shared-animal collaborators can find you. You may optionally secure your account with Sign in with Apple; if you do, Apple shares your name and email address (your real email, or Apple’s private “Hide My Email” relay address) with us so we can recover your account on a new device. Your profile also includes a display name you choose.

b) Content you create (“Observation Content”). Free-text behavior descriptions, photos, video recordings, audio recordings, daily check-ins, animal profile details (name, species, breed, age, weight, notes, routine), and accuracy ratings/feedback you give. This is stored locally on your device. Some of it is also stored on our servers when you use a feature that requires it, specifically:

  • Animals and observations you share (via a team or an Animal ID) — the animal profile, its photo, and the behavior observations shared to that team/collaboration are stored on our servers so the people you approve can see them.
  • Animal profile photos and team logos you upload are stored in our media storage.
  • Contributed observations you share for community statistics (on by default; see Section 4 and the opt-out below). These are pseudonymous, not anonymous — linked to your anonymous account identifier so we can delete them on request.
  • Acoustic measurements derived from your recordings — fundamental frequency, spectral centroid and spread, call rate, duration, normalised spectral shape — contributed to shared species reference data only if you switch on Atlas contribution (off by default; Section 4). The recordings themselves stay on your device.
  • Contribution-reliability data. If you contribute, we keep counts of how often your contributed measurements were corroborated or contradicted by other independent contributions, and a resulting reliability value. This governs how much weight your contributions carry in shared reference data and helps us detect deliberate distortion. Your numbers are private to you; other people only ever see a general standing label.
  • Achievements/badges you earn are synced so they survive a reinstall.

When you request an interpretation, what is sent off your device depends on the type:

  • Text, photos, and video are transmitted to our AI processor to generate the interpretation. (For video, we extract still frames on your device and send those frames.)
  • Audio recordings are processed on your device into rough metadata only (approximate recording length and a loudness estimate derived from file size) — the raw audio file is not transmitted to the AI processor; it stays on your device unless you delete it. Only that metadata and any text context you add are sent.

c) Teams and team clients (business/professional users). If you create or join a team, we store your team membership, role, and the team’s shared animals and observations. If you use the client-management feature, we store the client records you enter — which may include a client’s name, phone number, and email address. If you enter another person’s contact details, you are responsible for having a lawful basis to do so; you act as the controller of that client data and Fauna acts as your processor (a data-processing addendum is available on request).

d) Device and app data. App settings, subscription tier/entitlement state, and locally stored counters (e.g. monthly log counts). Subscription status is managed through Apple’s App Store / Google Play and your RevenueCat-validated entitlement; RevenueCat receives your purchase and entitlement history tied to your account identifier. We do not receive your full payment-card details.

e) Push notification token (if you enable notifications). We store your device’s push token (linked to your account) to deliver reminders/alerts you have opted into.

f) Diagnostic / crash data. To keep the app reliable, if the app hits an error we send a crash report to our servers (Supabase) containing a technical error message, a code stack trace, the platform, and the app version, linked to your anonymous account identifier. These reports are stripped of your content — they never include your notes, observations, animal names, or media — and are used solely to diagnose and fix bugs.

g) Product-usage analytics. To understand how the app is used and improve it, we collect content-free interaction events — e.g. which screens you view, when you save a log or check-in, when you view the paywall or complete a purchase — with lightweight properties (a screen name, your subscription tier, counts, booleans), linked to your anonymous account identifier. These events never contain your behavior text, notes, animal names, emails, or media. This is on by default; you can opt out anytime in Settings → Privacy. We use first-party analytics only — no third-party analytics SDK.

h) Safety & moderation data. To operate our safety tools, we store the list of users you block and any reports you submit about content or another user (your account identifier, the target, and the reason you provide). We use this to hide blocked users from you and to review and act on reports; a report identifies the user or content you reported so our team can review it. You can see and undo your blocks in Settings → Privacy → Blocked Users.

We do not intentionally collect special-category data about you. Because Observation Content is free text, please do not enter sensitive personal information about identifiable people.

Community observations (on by default — how to opt out). By default, Fauna contributes the behaviour note you wrote and the interpretation the AI returned, with the species and date, to build aggregate community statistics. We do not attach your name or your animal’s name to a contributed observation, and your photos, audio and video are not included — though an observation you have also shared to a team or a collaborator remains connected to that animal’s profile for those people. This data is pseudonymous, not anonymous: it is linked to your anonymous account identifier, which is exactly what allows us to find and delete it when you ask. Because your note is free text, please don’t type anything into it you would not want to appear in a shared record. You can turn it off at any time in Settings → Privacy; turning it off stops future contribution. Because this is on by default with an opt-out, we rely on legitimate interests for it (with your right to object), not opt-in consent. Note: the EU/EEA is excluded from distribution at launch. If we re-enable EU distribution, we will move community contribution to opt-in consent to meet GDPR consent standards.

Atlas contribution (OFF by default — opt-in only). Separately, you can switch on Atlas contribution. If you do, Fauna contributes measurements computed from your recordings — for example pitch, duration, spectral shape and call rate — together with the species you selected, the context you chose from a list, the date, and your device class and app version. We use them to build and publish shared reference data about how animals of each species vocalise. Your recordings themselves are never included, and neither are your free-text notes. Before any records are shared outside Fauna we remove your name, your account identifier and your animal’s name and replace the identifier with a code; that makes them pseudonymised, not anonymous, because we can still trace a record back to you in order to delete it — so we continue to treat them as your personal data and your rights over them continue to apply. We reserve the word anonymous for published aggregate statistics, which contain no individual record. The lawful basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)); we record which version of this policy you consented to. Two separate things, and the difference matters: (a) turning Atlas contribution off in Settings → Privacy stops all further contribution immediately, and leaves the measurements you have already contributed in the shared reference data; (b) Settings → Privacy → Remove My Contributions is the action that retracts — it takes every measurement of yours out of every published figure, one tap, automatically, with no email and no one reviewing it. Contributions are kept permanently unless you use (b), and that is part of what you agree to when you switch contribution on — the switch itself says so. The reason is that this data is pooled: a shared species profile that shrank every time one person left would misrepresent the work of everyone who stayed. Deleting your account does not retract them either. We ask you one question during that flow — whether to permanently remove the data you contributed to community science — and you have to answer yes; the default is to leave it. Anything kept after an account is deleted first has your account identifier, the link to the original observation and the recording’s acoustic fingerprint removed, is reduced to a per-contributor median, and has its date coarsened to the month, so it can no longer be traced back to you. Observations contributed before this version of the policy, under the earlier community-statistics basis, are not included in the Atlas.

Your recordings stay on your device. Today, no audio file leaves your device: when you ask for an analysis, only measurements computed on your device plus the text you wrote are sent. If we add a feature that needs to send a recording off your device — such as identifying a species from audio — we will ask you for that recording, at that moment, before it is sent, and we will update this policy before the feature ships.

Product analytics (on by default — how to opt out). Fauna records content-free usage events to see which features help and where people get stuck, so we can improve the app. These events never include your content, your animal’s name, or media. Turn them off anytime in Settings → Privacy; we rely on legitimate interests (with your right to object). As with research sharing, if we re-enable EU distribution we will move analytics to opt-in consent.

We do not carry out solely automated decision-making that produces legal or similarly significant effects about you under Article 22 GDPR. AI interpretations are informational outputs about your animal, are not decisions about you, and are not a substitute for professional advice.

5. Daily check-ins and the “Guardian” pattern read

Fauna offers a daily check-in (a short, structured pulse on your animal or colony) and a Living Dossier with an AI pattern read (“the Guardian”) that looks at your check-in history over time and surfaces steady baselines and changes worth watching.

  • Your check-in data is stored locally on your device. It is mirrored to our servers only for animals you share (team or Animal ID), so collaborators can see the shared record.
  • The Guardian pattern read sends a short text summary of your check-in trends (and, if you added them, your recent notes) to our AI processor to produce the read. It follows the same AI-processing terms as Section 6.
  • The check-in and Guardian features are non-diagnostic. They read your own records; they do not diagnose disease and are not veterinary advice. For any health concern, consult a licensed veterinarian (or, for a colony, a mentor/inspection). See our Terms of Service.

6. AI processing and third parties

To generate interpretations and pattern reads, the Observation Content you submit (text, photos, and video frames — and for audio, only on-device-derived metadata plus your text context, not the raw audio file, per Section 3) is transmitted from your device, authenticated as your account, to:

  • Cloudflare, Inc. — hosts the processing proxy through which requests pass. See cloudflare.com/privacypolicy
  • Anthropic, PBC — the AI provider that generates the interpretation. See anthropic.com/legal/privacy

We also use:

  • Supabase — hosts our database, authentication (your anonymous account), and media storage.
  • RevenueCat — validates your subscription purchases/entitlements.
  • Expo / Apple / Google — app delivery, over-the-air updates, and push notification delivery.

This data leaves your device when you use these features. We pass your content to these providers solely to produce the output or run the feature you requested. We do not control, and cannot guarantee, how the AI providers log, cache, or retain data on their own systems; please review their respective policies. We instruct our providers not to use submitted content to train their models where such controls are available to us, but you should not rely on this for confidential material.

Our product analytics are first-party and content-free (see Section 4) — we do not use any third-party analytics or advertising SDKs, and we do not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.

8. Where your data is stored and for how long

  • Observation Content on your device stays there until you delete it (delete a log, an animal, or use “Clear All” in Settings) or uninstall the app.
  • Data on our servers (your anonymous account/profile, shared animals and observations, team data and client records, media you uploaded, achievements, push token, and crash reports) is retained while your account is active. You can delete your account and all associated server data at any time in Settings → Delete Account & Data; if you own a team, ownership passes to your next-highest-ranking member. Uninstalling the app does not by itself delete server data — use Delete Account & Data (or email us). Acoustic measurements you contributed to the shared species statistics are the one exception, and it is deliberate. They are kept, because a shared species profile that shrank every time one person left would misrepresent the work of everyone who stayed. During the deletion flow we ask you plainly whether to permanently remove them, and you have to answer yes — the default is to leave them. Anything we keep has the account identifier, the link to the original observation and the audio fingerprint removed and its date reduced to the month first, so it cannot be linked back to you. Separately and at any time, Settings → Privacy → Remove My Contributions takes every measurement of yours out of every published figure. It is automatic and immediate — no email and no human review — and it is the action that retracts a contribution. Turning the contribution switch off is not the same thing: it stops new contributions and leaves existing ones in place.
  • Content sent to our AI processor for an interpretation is retained by that processor according to their retention policies (see Section 6), not ours.
  • Subscription/entitlement records are retained by Apple/Google/RevenueCat per their policies and our need to honor your purchases.
  • Crash reports are retained only as long as needed to diagnose stability issues.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you have rights over your personal data.

EEA / UK / Switzerland (GDPR / UK GDPR): access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and the right to withdraw consent at any time. You can exercise access and erasure directly in the app: your on-device content via the in-app delete tools, and your server-side data via Settings → Delete Account & Data. You can object to community/research contribution at any time via Settings → Privacy. For anything else we or our processors hold, contact us at fauna.app.support@gmail.com. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with your local supervisory authority.

California (CCPA/CPRA): the right to know, delete, correct, and to opt out of “sale”/“sharing.” We do not sell or share your personal information as those terms are defined under the CPRA, and we do not process it for cross-context behavioral advertising. We will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights.

To make a request, email fauna.app.support@gmail.com. We will verify your request and respond within the timeframe required by applicable law (generally within one month under GDPR; 45 days under the CCPA, extendable).

10. Children

Fauna is not directed to children. You must be at least 13 years old (16 in the EEA/UK) to use Fauna. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children below the applicable age. If you believe a child has provided us data, contact fauna.app.support@gmail.com and we will delete it.
